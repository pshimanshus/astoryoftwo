## Core Definition

A successful @a.storyof.two carousel makes a cold viewer recognize a
relationship truth before they admire the art.

It is not successful because it is pretty, romantic, polished, or technically
complete. It is successful when a stranger can immediately think:

```text
this is us
this is them
I need to send this
I want to save this because it explains a kind of love I know
```

The gold carousel worked because it became a public identity mirror with
private receipts. The viewer did not need to know Aachu and Zuv first. They
understood the relationship dynamic first, then the Aachu/Zuv specifics made it
feel observed, funny, desi, and emotionally safe.

The repeatable machine is:

```text
public contradiction
-> concrete couple receipts
-> active partner response
-> emotional reversal
-> tender taggable thesis
```

For @a.storyof.two, that usually translates to:

```text
universal relationship truth
-> Aachu-specific spark
-> visible proof
-> Zuv's active steadiness
-> earned send/save payoff
```

## Implementation Principle

This standard must not become a small keyword checklist.

The pipeline should carry it as an open agent-alignment contract: concept,
story, copy, visual, prompt, and final QA agents must explain how their choices
serve the real goals. Agents may choose any hook, scene system, motif, or slide
count that gets a cold viewer to the right recognition. A hook can be quiet,
funny, cinematic, Hinglish, visual-first, or structurally unusual if it still
creates a public doorway into the relationship truth.

Use deterministic checks only for hard structural misses:

- the object, place, outfit, or aesthetic is the whole premise instead of
  evidence;
- no agent records how the concept aligns to the success goals;
- Story-Selling, golden-theme, story-director, visual-room, or visual-plan
  gates are missing or below threshold;
- final image prompts do not carry the success goals into scene-first visual
  generation.

Identity matching remains a separate hard gate. Do not let face/likeness
review replace creative success, and do not let creative success excuse
identity drift. Both must pass.

## What Success Means

Success has four layers.

### 1. Audience Success

The post creates send/save/tag behavior, not only passive likes.

Strong signals:

- partner tags in comments;
- comments that say "this is us" or tag one person with no explanation;
- saves because the final line feels emotionally true;
- shares because the post gives language to a real couple dynamic;
- viewers remember the behavior, not only the artwork.

The gold carousel showed this clearly: the comments sample was dominated by
partner mentions, which means the carousel made viewers point at someone.

Weak signals:

- "beautiful";
- "nice art";
- "prompt please";
- generic heart comments with no relationship recognition;
- admiration for the illustration before recognition of the story.

If the artifact makes people notice AI output before they feel the couple
dynamic, the carousel has failed its first job.

### 2. Creative Success

The deck is a story sequence, not a set of nice lines.

A successful carousel has:

- a two-second public hook;
- a small emotional obstacle;
- behavior-based proof beats;
- one physically funny, surprising, or highly visual receipt;
- Zuv doing care as action, not mood;
- a turn from joke, friction, or surface detail into tenderness;
- a final line that would still work if reposted as a quote, but only because
  the previous slides earned it.

The viewer should be able to follow the deck as silent panels: scene, cut,
reaction, escalation, role, payoff. Text helps orientation; it should not carry
the whole story.

### 3. Brand Success

The carousel builds ownable relationship IP.

The channel is not competing as a generic couple page, travel page, quote page,
or AI illustration page. Its strongest lane is:

```text
warm, funny, desi relationship storytelling where small behaviors become
evidence of love
```

A successful carousel strengthens that world. It makes Aachu and Zuv feel like
recurring characters with a recognizable rhythm:

- she brings plot, appetite, emotion, color, drama, longing, and expressive
  truth;
- he brings patience, timing, grounded humor, protection, translation, and
  active steadiness;
- the relationship turns ordinary objects and moments into receipts of care.

Brand-safe does not mean bland. It means affectionate, culturally rooted,
non-mean, non-shaming, and easy for a partner, food, travel, home, fashion, or
gifting brand to enter as a care object inside the story.

### 4. Production Success

The package is only truly successful when the final artifact is publishable.

Creative approval is not enough. The carousel must also pass the C-layer gates:

- Story-Selling score: 28/30 or higher;
- Golden Theme score: 28/30 or higher;
- concept-selection record exists with 5-10 variants and a selector verdict;
- post-copy visual room exists after copy approval;
- visual debate returns GO;
- visual-plan-quality returns GO per slide;
- identity-consistency review passes before generation;
- final 4:5 images are native outputs;
- final 9:16 Reels/Stories images are separate native outputs, not resized
  derivatives;
- visual QA confirms identity, text, style, scene logic, and continuity;
- final audit is PASS or an explicit PASS_WITH_NOTES with no identity or final
  image blocker.

Identity drift is never a minor note. If Aachu and Zuv do not look like the
selected identity bundle, the final output is not successful.

## How A Successful Carousel Looks

### The Hook

Slide 1 is a public contradiction or recognition point.

It should be understandable without the caption, without private context, and
without needing the viewer to care about the photo yet.

Strong hook shapes:

- rejects a socially approved ideal;
- names a relationship pattern people know;
- makes one partner think of the other;
- creates a curiosity gap that slide 2 must answer;
- feels sendable before it feels poetic.

Weak hook shapes:

- a private quote with no setup;
- an object as the premise;
- a place as the premise;
- a soft mood line;
- a caption-like sentence;
- generic romance language.

The hook test:

```text
Can a stranger understand the relationship tension in under two seconds?
```

If not, repair.

### The Middle Slides

The middle slides are receipts.

They should prove the hook with specific behavior. The gold carousel had
receipt density: crying while saying she is fine, leaving without shoes, ten
moods before breakfast, and his smile that treats it as normal.

Future carousels need similar proof density. The proof can be softer, funnier,
more cinematic, or more daily-life, but it cannot be abstract.

Strong receipts:

- visible actions;
- body language;
- objects being used as care, not decoration;
- a phrase people have heard in their own home;
- a food, phone, money, outfit, travel, or household detail that reveals the
  relationship;
- one behavior that is funny because it is true.

Weak receipts:

- "waiting";
- "home";
- "softness";
- "care";
- "vibes";
- "magic";
- "beautiful memory";
- any object that does not prove a relationship behavior.

Objects, outfits, food, cities, phones, wallets, flowers, bags, chai, and shoes
are evidence. They are not the thesis.

### The Partner Role

Zuv must have an active emotional role.

He is not a passive good husband, a saintly background figure, or a man who
only waits while Aachu performs the story. His steadiness must become visible
behavior.

Strong Zuv actions:

- notices what she is not saying;
- orders extra before she admits hunger;
- stands close enough without taking over;
- holds space in a crowd;
- joins the joke instead of judging it;
- carries the awkward part quietly;
- protects her softness without shrinking her;
- smiles because he has chosen this exact life, not because he is absent.

If Zuv can be removed and the story still works, the concept fails.

### The Emotional Turn

A successful carousel changes feeling before it ends.

Common strong turns:

- joke to tenderness;
- chaos to safety;
- surface habit to proof of care;
- misread behavior to deeper need;
- private weirdness to shared home;
- independence to being held without being controlled;
- nonsense to belonging.

The final slide should not merely restate the hook in softer words. It should
reveal what the previous receipts were really about.

### The Final Line

The final line is the send/save thesis.

It should answer:

```text
If someone sends this to their partner, what are they really saying?
```

Strong answers:

- you make my chaos feel safe;
- you understand what I mean before I say it;
- you join my nonsense instead of judging it;
- you care without making me feel small;
- you learned the real me, not the polished version.

Weak answer:

```text
this is beautiful
```

Beautiful is not enough. The final line needs identity pressure.

## Slide Architecture

Use the number of slides the story needs. Five slides are useful, but not a
law. Do not compress a real story until the bridge, proof trail, Zuv response,
and emotional payoff become thin.

### Five-Slide Shape

| Slide | Job | Question It Answers |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Universal hook | What relationship truth is this about? |
| 2 | Aachu spark | What specific version of the truth lives here? |
| 3 | Concrete receipt | What behavior proves it? |
| 4 | Zuv action | What does love do in response? |
| 5 | Tender thesis | What can the viewer send or save? |

### Seven-Slide Shape

Use this when the idea needs more escalation.

| Slide | Job | Question It Answers |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Contradiction hook | What expected ideal are we rejecting? |
| 2 | First proof | What did he actually choose? |
| 3 | Physical comic receipt | What makes it instantly visual? |
| 4 | Pattern proof | Is this a one-time thing or a relationship rhythm? |
| 5 | Partner response | How does he hold it? |
| 6 | Emotional turn | What was the joke really about? |
| 7 | Taggable thesis | What line travels outside the post? |

### Daily-Human Story Shape

For comedy, chores, food, money, phone, home, or daily-life posts, storyboard
first and write copy last.

The deck should feel like:

```text
scene
-> first instinct
-> bad idea / friction
-> reaction
-> reversal
-> consequence
-> tiny emotional proof
```

Use text that belongs inside the frame: a context line, thought bubble, speech
bubble, tiny reply, or deliberate silence. Delete fake-clever poster text and
relationship-lesson lines if the scene can carry them.

## Visual Standard

The house look is soft hand-drawn desi storybook illustration on warm paper:
imperfect black linework, matte muted colors, expressive recurring Aachu/Zuv
faces, generous whitespace, and small alive details like blush marks, hearts,
reaction ticks, motion lines, thought bubbles, or one care object.

Visual success is not decoration. Every slide should show one relationship beat.

Strong visual direction:

- one clear scene per slide;
- expressions and hands do real storytelling work;
- camera angle changes create swipe momentum;
- props are few and meaningful;
- text has safe negative space;
- Aachu/Zuv identity remains consistent;
- 4:5 and 9:16 compositions are planned separately;
- the illustration feels warm and human, not glossy, stock, or generic AI art.

Weak visual direction:

- atmospheric romance without behavior;
- repeated chai/home/car/table scenes by default;
- one prop motif repeated across every slide;
- paper artifacts, receipts, labels, or UI metaphors as the main system;
- over-rendered backgrounds;
- characters who look generic;
- text-heavy quote-card layout;
- visuals that explain the object but not the relationship.

The visual rule:

```text
Prove behavior before mood.
```

## Copy Standard

On-slide copy should usually be 3-8 words. One slide, one job.

Use Hinglish only when it sharpens recognition, not as decoration. Public copy
should default to "he", "she", "we", "us", or relationship roles. Do not force
Aachu/Zuv or Anchal/Himanshu into public slide text unless the story needs it.

Strong copy feels:

- spoken;
- short;
- observed;
- visual;
- affectionate;
- easy to send.

Weak copy feels:

- written before the scene exists;
- explanatory;
- too poetic too early;
- generic couple-goals;
- self-help advice;
- mean toward one partner;
- dependent on private context.

## Caption Standard

The caption should expand the theme, not repeat the slides.

Required behavior:

- first line reopens the universal relationship truth;
- middle lines add more specific receipts;
- one spoken Hinglish line is allowed if natural;
- the emotional interpretation is clear;
- the ending creates a natural send/save/comment reason.

A hard CTA works only when the idea already deserves the send. Do not use
"send this to..." to manufacture shareability from a weak concept.

## Success Scorecard

Before packaging, score the carousel as a creative object and as a publishable
artifact.

| Dimension | Pass Standard |
|---|---|
| Reader identity mirror | A stranger can recognize themselves or their partner immediately. |
| Public hook | Slide 1 is clear in under two seconds. |
| Emotional obstacle | There is friction, misread, cost, or desire. |
| Concrete receipts | Middle slides contain behavior-based proof, not abstract mood. |
| Physical visual proof | At least one receipt is instantly drawable or physically funny. |
| Aachu role | She brings specific spark, not generic wife/girl energy. |
| Zuv role | His care is visible action, not passive patience. |
| Emotional reversal | The deck turns from surface to meaning. |
| Final thesis | The final line can be sent or saved without backstory. |
| Visual scene clarity | The deck works as silent panels. |
| Caption expansion | The caption adds proof and distribution reason. |
| Identity continuity | Faces, posture, outfits, and couple likeness pass QA. |
| Native outputs | Separate 4:5 and 9:16 final images exist. |

Decision rule:

- GO: all critical dimensions pass and both Story-Selling and Golden Theme
  scores are 28/30 or higher.
- REPAIR: one or more dimensions are weak but the emotional machine is alive.
- REWORK: the premise is private, abstract, derivative, or visually thin.
- STOP: no obstacle, no active Zuv role, generic couple dynamic, object-first
  premise, mean framing, or unfixable identity/art failure.

## Hard Fails

Block or repair before packaging if any of these are true:

- slide 1 needs private context;
- the hook is mainly about an object, outfit, location, or aesthetic;
- there is no emotional obstacle;
- the middle slides are only pleasant lines;
- Aachu is mocked, shamed, or reduced to "too much";
- Zuv is removable, passive, saintly, or only waiting;
- the ending is an unearned quote;
- the carousel would receive "beautiful" but not "this is us";
- a viewer notices AI/artifacts before relationship truth;
- image generation starts without identity review;
- final art has identity drift;
- 9:16 outputs are derived from 4:5 instead of generated natively.

## Calibration Examples

### Gold: He Didn't Marry Peace

Why it passed:

- contradiction hook: "peace" is a socially approved ideal;
- specific receipts: tears, shoes, moods, breakfast;
- active Zuv role: he smiles like this is normal;
- emotional turn: the chaos becomes safe;
- final thesis: "calm enough for your chaos" is taggable and portable.

This is the current 30/30 reference.

### Moderate: He Didn't Marry Low Maintenance

Why it partially worked:

- close to the gold architecture;
- recognizable emotional subject;
- some concrete proof.

Why it weakened:

- "low maintenance" is colder and more dating-market coded than "peace";
- it repeated the gold territory too soon;
- proof density and physical comedy were weaker;
- final line was tender but less tag-ready.

Lesson: copying the chassis without the same receipt density does not recreate
the engine.

### Weak: Some Days Make Him Want Silence

Why it failed:

- slide 1 starts from his exhaustion, not a public identity mirror;
- the premise risks making Aachu feel like labor;
- Zuv's action is internal and soft rather than visible;
- visuals read as mood and AI polish more than relationship proof;
- comments did not show tag behavior.

Lesson: soft, true, and pretty can still be unsendable.

### Useful But Under-Hooked: Main Kar Lungi

Why it had promise:

- the phrase is culturally real;
- the translation idea is strong;
- Zuv's care can be active and respectful.

Why it needed repair:

- slide 1 is a private phrase before it is a public hook;
- the core action is visually subtle;
- the final thesis risks sounding like advice.

Lesson: a great private line still needs a stranger doorway.

## The One Question

Before any future carousel proceeds, ask:

```text
Would a stranger send this to their partner because it explains their
relationship, while Aachu and Zuv still feel like the only two people it could
be about?
```

If the answer is no, keep working.
