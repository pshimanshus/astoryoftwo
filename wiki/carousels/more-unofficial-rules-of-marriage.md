## Slide Flow

- Slide 1: More unofficial rules of marriage
that nobody tells you - Friday evening in their bedroom-living-room threshold. A half-packed suitcase is open. Aachu searches through clothes while Zuv crosses the room carrying two mugs and carefully stepping over a charging cable. They occupy different parts of the frame but exchange one familiar glance. The room is mildly unfinished, never dirty or cluttered.
- Slide 2: Rule no. 1
“Should we order something?”
is not a question.
The decision has already been made. - Aachu and Zuv sit at opposite ends of the sofa during packing. One performs a casual asking gesture while the other is already selecting dishes on the phone placed between them. Both lean toward the same phone, revealing that the question was ceremonial rather than undecided.
- Slide 3: Rule no. 2
If one person asks, “Kahan hai?”
The other must find it
without being told what “it” is. - During packing, Zuv urgently searches around the suitcase for the room keys while the keys already dangle visibly from the fingers of his raised searching hand. Aachu pauses beside the suitcase and silently points at his hand, familiar rather than mocking.
- Slide 4: Rule no. 3
Old screenshots
do not have an expiry date.
They can be presented as evidence
at any time. - Zuv confidently gestures that he never said something. Aachu calmly turns her phone toward him with old message evidence already prepared. His free hand freezes mid-defence and his expression collapses into a sheepish smile; she remains composed, not triumphant or hostile.
- Slide 5: Rule no. 4
The person who did not pack
will always ask,
“Charger rakh liya?”
Usually when you’re already in the cab. - They are already entering a waiting cab. Aachu has the travel bags and turns sharply when Zuv, carrying almost nothing, asks about the charger. Both look back through their still-open home doorway, where the single charger remains visibly plugged into the wall. The forgotten object creates the consequence.
- Slide 6: Rule no. 5
Wi-Fi starts working
the moment your spouse enters the room.
Nobody knows why. - Inside the hotel, Aachu crouches near a router after trying every fix. Zuv casually enters through the bathroom doorway doing nothing. At that exact moment the router's lights become steady and the nearby laptop changes from a disconnected symbol to a simple connected symbol. Aachu slowly looks up at him, personally betrayed by technology; he gives a helpless shrug.
- Slide 7: Rule no. 6
Midnight hunger
is never one person’s emergency.
The entire house gets activated. - Blue hotel night after one person admits hunger. Show the consequence rather than the whisper: both are fully activated. Aachu sits forward comparing food options while Zuv pulls on slippers and empties their remaining snack bag onto the bed between them. Their separate tasks form one shared operation.
- Slide 8: Rule no. 7
Every unnecessary purchase
begins immediately after
a serious conversation
about saving money. - The next afternoon, Aachu and Zuv leave a local market carrying one beautiful but obviously impractical oversized lamp together. A folded budget note sticks harmlessly from a pocket. Both look from the object to each other with equal guilt and delight; the purchase physically requires their cooperation.
- Slide 9: Rule no. 8
Nobody is actually in charge.
Both just keep showing up.
Usually saying, “Maine bola tha.” - Back home, Aachu and Zuv try to carry the travel bags and oversized lamp through the doorway together. The lamp begins to slip; both catch and stabilize it in the same instant. Once safe, each points lightly at the other with a free finger and they break into laughter, shoulder-to-shoulder. This returns to slide 1's home geography, but now their movement is synchronized and the shared object connects them.
