## Core Story

Aachu is looking for her Dot & Key Barrier Repair tube.

It is not where it should be.

She checks the drawer. The skincare pouch. The wardrobe. The bathroom. Every
place where a tiny blue tube can disappear.

Her strawberry face is slowly becoming a full investigation.

She starts suspecting Zuv.

But he has not stolen it.

He knows the boring household answer: she left the old one at her parents'
place, and he had already ordered a backup before it became an emergency.

That is the love story:

```text
She lives dramatically in the house.
He quietly runs the inventory.
```

The brand integration works because the product is not an ad prop. It is one of
the real household things he tracks because she uses it, likes it, and will
need it before she remembers she will need it.

## Alternate Endings

Most creator-native:

```text
She lives here. He runs inventory.
```

Softer:

```text
She forgets. He restocks.
```

More brand-pitch friendly:

```text
The backup was already home.
```

## Why This Fits Better

- The missing product creates a teasing game without making Zuv a thief.
- The search sequence gives the carousel movement: pouch -> drawer -> bathroom
  -> wardrobe -> Zuv -> backup drawer -> parents' place reveal.
- The payoff is not "he is perfect"; it is funny domestic competence.
- Aachu's strawberry face has an active role: she becomes detective, not
  problem.
- Dot & Key fits as household inventory evidence, not a forced romance symbol.

## Brand Integration Notes

- Hero SKU stays Dot & Key Barrier Repair Moisturizer.
- Product label must read `DOT & KEY` and `Barrier Repair` on slides 6-7.
- Avoid implying the product cures or changes her face.
- The product is valuable because Aachu already uses it and Zuv tracks the
  household stock.
- If this becomes a pitch deck for Dot & Key, the hook is: "a creator-native
  relationship carousel where Barrier Repair appears as the product husbands
  learn to restock before it runs out."
