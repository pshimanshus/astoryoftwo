## Slide Flow

- 1: some people change the weather inside you. - A warm interior scene with one partner arriving emotionally crowded by the day: bag dropped, phone in hand, shoulders tense, the room feeling slightly compressed. The other partner is present nearby but not heroic or therapist-like, simply noticing with gentle attention. The feeling should be weather-like through light and posture, not literal clouds.
- 2: before them, everything feels too loud. - A closer, slightly crowded frame: notifications glow on the phone, keys or bag are messy, shoulders lifted, eyes tired. The other partner is not solving anything yet; the room and objects make the loudness visible without using literal sound effects.
- 3: the same thoughts. the same worries. the same life. - A wider room scene where the practical world is still visible: phone, small bills or notebook without readable text, laundry or daily objects, unfinished cup. One partner is mid-thought, the other sits close enough to be present. Nothing is fixed yet.
- 4: then they sit next to you, and somehow... - The partner simply sits beside them on the bed, sofa, or floor edge. No advice pose, no lecture. Hands are close but not forced; eye-line softens; body distance reduces a little. The scene should prove presence as the action.
- 5: your chest stops arguing with the world. - A gentle mid-hour beat: the tense partner is now exhaling, maybe leaning back or laughing softly while still holding the phone face down. The other partner's presence makes the body loosen; hands unclench, shoulders drop. Use chest/shoulder posture as the receipt.
- 6: nothing is fixed.
but everything feels softer. - The key proof slide: same phone/problem remains in the frame, but the emotional weather has changed. The couple is calmer, closer, maybe sharing a small laugh or quiet side-by-side silence. The outside world is unchanged; light and posture are softer.
- 7: maybe love is just this.
one person making life feel bearable again. - A final quiet wide shot after the hour: both people settled close in the same room, ordinary objects still around, but the frame breathes. One small shared gesture, like shoulder against shoulder, fingers touching, or a shared smile, lands the emotional thesis without grand romance.

it started with one ordinary moment.
