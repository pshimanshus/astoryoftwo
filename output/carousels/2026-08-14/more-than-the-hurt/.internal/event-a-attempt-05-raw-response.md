# Event A — Copy-Hidden Storyboard Read

## Card 1

- **Who / where:** Aachu is seated at a two-place dinner table at night; Zuv has stopped in the dining-room doorway with his phone and keys.
- **Visible action:** She remains beside an untouched meal while he pauses on arrival rather than crossing to the empty chair.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** The relationship reads as strained and suspended. Aachu appears hurt or resigned after waiting; Zuv appears newly confronted by the consequence of his absence.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, Aachu likely prepared or waited through a planned dinner that Zuv missed or reached very late. Next, he may put away the arrival objects and try to engage, while the empty chair remains the immediate barrier.
- **Visible proof:** Two set places, the untouched second plate, burned-low candle, cold meal, Aachu's gaze at the empty chair, Zuv's gaze toward her, and the chair centered between them.
- **Uncertainties:** The reason for Zuv's timing is unknowable. He could be arriving late, returning after leaving, or pausing during an interrupted departure; remorse is plausible but not yet proved.

## Card 2

- **Who / where:** Both remain in the same dining room moments later, viewed low through the empty chair.
- **Visible action:** Zuv deliberately puts his phone face-down beside the keys while staying on the far side of the chair.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** This reads as a tentative act of attention or de-escalation, but not yet repair. Aachu watches cautiously; Zuv is available enough to remove a distraction while still physically separated.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, he entered holding the phone and keys and saw the missed dinner. Next, either a difficult exchange begins or Aachu withdraws because his first gesture is not enough.
- **Visible proof:** The phone changes from held to face-down, the keys are already set aside, his other hand hangs open, their sightline crosses the chair slats, and no touch or chair movement closes the gap.
- **Uncertainties:** Turning a phone face-down can signal attention, shame, avoidance, or simple habit. The chair is a strong visual barrier, but its emotional meaning depends partly on the surrounding sequence.

## Card 3

- **Who / where:** Aachu is alone in a dim hallway later that night beside a narrow console.
- **Visible action:** She turns the last upright small frame face-down beside two already face-down frames.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** The state reads as controlled withdrawal: Aachu is choosing not to look at something emotionally charged, without smashing, discarding, or packing it away.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, the dining-room encounter likely failed to resolve the hurt. Next, the night gives way to a morning decision about whether this act of withdrawal will remain or be partly reversed.
- **Visible proof:** Her hand is attached to and actively rotating the final frame; her gaze stays on it; exactly three frames end face-down; none is broken or removed; she is visibly alone.
- **Uncertainties:** Because no frame image is visible, the frames cannot independently prove that they contain couple memories. Turning them down could also mean privacy, grief, or avoidance of any personally significant images.

## Card 4

- **Who / where:** The following morning, Zuv sits in the chair that had been empty while Aachu pauses in the dining-room doorway.
- **Visible action:** He waits in the charged seat with open, separate hands as she decides whether to enter the room.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** Zuv reads as available and possibly accountable; Aachu reads as guarded. This is a reopened encounter, not a reconciliation.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, Aachu withdrew and turned the frames down; Zuv either remained or returned to occupy the place whose absence defined the night. Next, Aachu may stay at the threshold, leave, or choose to engage.
- **Visible proof:** The formerly empty chair is now occupied, the cold plate and dead candle remain, phone and keys stay face-down, Zuv's hands are open on his thighs, and open floor separates him from Aachu at the threshold.
- **Uncertainties:** His seated waiting does not by itself prove apology or remorse. Her threshold position does not reveal whether she is entering, stopping, or preparing to turn away.

## Card 5

- **Who / where:** Aachu and Zuv sit opposite each other across the small table in the sparse morning dining room.
- **Visible action:** Aachu is actively lifting one palm-sized frame from face-down toward upright while already looking directly at Zuv.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** This reads as a cautious, voluntary reopening by Aachu and mutual acknowledgment by both people. It does not read as completed reconciliation: their torsos remain separate, the tabletop gap is intact, their hands stay to themselves, and there is no romantic lean or touch.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, all three frames were turned down and Zuv waited for Aachu to choose whether to engage. Next, she may finish setting the frame upright and the pair may begin talking, while the other two frames remain unresolved.
- **Visible proof:** Her attached right hand supports the frame at 45 degrees, its bottom edge remains planted, and the object is visibly between its prior face-down state and a future upright state. Her shifted gaze makes the lift feel chosen in relation to Zuv; his open hands, the gap, and the absence of touch prevent the moment from collapsing into a posed romantic resolution.
- **Uncertainties:** Direct mutual eye contact could, in isolation, look like a reconciled portrait, and the plain frame back does not identify its contents. The active mid-lift and the deliberately unreconciled blocking are the concrete evidence that defeat that reading in sequence.
- **Specific gate:** **YES. Card 5 reads as an active voluntary state change, not as an already-reconciled gaze.** The frame is visibly in motion under Aachu's hand, only halfway restored, while the couple's bodies and hands retain unresolved distance.

## Card 6

- **Who / where:** Both are seated at adjacent sides of the same table on the dining-room/hallway axis in clear morning light.
- **Visible action:** They begin a restrained exchange while the once-empty chair has been pulled out of its dividing position.
- **Relationship / emotional state:** The pair read as cautiously re-engaged and willing to remain present. The emotional state is partial repair in progress, not a clean reset.
- **Probable before / next:** Before, Aachu made the first reversible move by lifting one frame and meeting Zuv's gaze. Next, the conversation may continue and determine whether the remaining two face-down frames are restored; no outcome is guaranteed.
- **Visible proof:** The chair no longer bisects them, body distance has reduced without touch, their sightline is mutual, one frame is upright while two remain face-down, and the cold meal, dead candle, phone, and keys remain as unresolved evidence rather than disappearing.
- **Uncertainties:** A still image may not prove that words are being spoken, so the frame could read as sustained eye contact rather than literal conversation. The content and outcome of any exchange remain unknowable.

## Sequence read

After a missed dinner leaves Aachu waiting and Zuv outside the empty chair, he sets distractions aside but she withdraws; by morning he occupies the charged seat and waits until she actively begins to reverse one act of withdrawal, allowing a guarded conversation without pretending the hurt is fully resolved.

## Setup / payoff links

- The central empty chair in Cards 1–2 becomes Zuv's accountability seat in Card 4, then moves out of its role as a divider in Card 6.
- Zuv's held phone and keys in Card 1 become inactive in Card 2 and remain put away through the morning, supporting sustained attention rather than a one-frame gesture.
- The cold meal and dying candle establish the missed occasion in Card 1; their persistence through Cards 4 and 6 keeps the original hurt materially present during repair.
- Three upright-to-face-down frame states culminate in Card 3; one frame is carried into the shared space, lifted mid-change in Card 5, and upright in Card 6 while two remain down, making the payoff partial and calibrated.
- Aachu's isolation in the hallway and her later pause at the doorway pay off in the final matched-axis view, where the space is shared and the former obstruction has shifted.

## Weakest frame

**Card 6 is the weakest frame.** “Beginning a restrained conversation” is less visually falsifiable in a still than the phone placement, frame turn, waiting posture, or mid-lift; without visible mouth movement or a conversational gesture, it can read as two people holding eye contact. It remains functional because the moved chair, reduced distance, one-upright/two-down frame state, and persistent dinner residue visibly complete the sequence's partial-release logic.

## Verdict

**PASS.** The sequence communicates a specific rupture, withdrawal, waiting, voluntary partial reopening, and cautious re-engagement through traceable actions, blocking, and object-state changes. Card 5 clears the named gate decisively: Aachu's hand-controlled, halfway frame lift is an ongoing and reversible action, while the no-touch, no-lean blocking keeps the eye contact from reading as a finished reconciliation.
