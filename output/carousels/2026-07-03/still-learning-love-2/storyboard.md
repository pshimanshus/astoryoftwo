# Still Learning Love

Healthy love is not proved by never fighting; it is proved by noticing the pattern sooner and choosing softness before the fight becomes the whole room.

## Slide Flow

- 1 / Cover: we talk about healthy love,
but we still learn it mid-fight. - Immediate cover contradiction: Aachu and Zuv are mid-fight in a lived-in room, verbally tense but not cruel. Their bodies are angled away, hands animated, brows tight, with tiny signs they still care: one cup between them, a blanket half-shared, feet still pointed toward each other. Make it active, not quiet poster sadness.
- 2 / Cold Open: we talk about patience,
but sometimes our tone arrives first. - Show the two verbally fighting or sharply talking over each other in a kitchen or small room. One partner points or lifts a hand mid-sentence while the other reacts, a cup/spoon/object landing too hard on the counter. The receipt is tone arriving before patience: visible mouths open, tense hands, not physical aggression.
- 3 / Mirror: we talk about listening,
but sometimes we just don't listen at all. - Stage a clear not-listening beat: both partners are in the same room but mentally elsewhere, one talking while the other looks away at a phone, cupboard, or floor; or both speak at once with crossed eye-lines. It should feel instantly recognizable: they are hearing sound, not receiving each other.
- 4 / Spine: we talk about giving space,
but still always irritate the hell out of each other. - Action over words: one partner tries to get space on a bed, sofa, or floor corner, while the other keeps hovering nearby and irritating them in a petty familiar way: poking, making faces, moving a pillow, tapping the door, or standing too close. Make it funny-irritating, not mean.
- 5 / Rhythm: we talk about saying sorry,
but never apologies when we should. - Show the apology failure actively: both partners are close enough to repair but stubbornly not apologizing. One holds a cup or folded arms; the other looks away with a wounded-but-proud face. The apology is available in the room but withheld. No peaceful tea apology; this beat is the stubborn silence after the fight.
- 6 / Turn: we talk about forever,
but we fight like we don't know each other. - The strongest fight receipt: late evening, both tired and emotional, standing/sitting apart like strangers in their own room. Their posture says they know each other deeply, but the fight has made them act unfamiliar. Use distance, crossed eye-lines, scattered blanket/cups, and one small shared object between them.
- 7 / Payoff: maybe love is noticing the pattern,
and choosing softness sooner. - Morning-after or after-fight payoff: the fight energy softens through action, not a grand hug. One partner reaches first, adjusts a blanket, moves closer, or offers a small touch; the other accepts. The room still has traces of the fight, but the body language shows they noticed the pattern sooner.
