# A Story of Two Copy Voice Bible

The copy style guide. Used with `anti-ai-slop-human-copy-filter`. The image
carries the joke; the copy carries the aftertaste.

> **Examples are calibration anchors, not reusable templates.** Do not copy
> example lines blindly. Use examples to understand the taste bar, then create a
> fresh line and scene for the current run.

# Research-Backed Copy Rule

Copy choices must be compared against actual winning copy mechanics in
`.agents/skills/astory/references/source_backed_winner_patterns.md`.

For each selected copy line, the run must record:
- which source-backed copy mechanic it uses (cite the pattern + shortcode),
- why the line is fresh,
- why it is sendable,
- why it does not merely copy the source,
- why it is not generic couple-account copy.

If the line is based only on generic taste and cannot cite a source-backed
mechanic → `RESEARCH_EVIDENCE_MISSING`.
If the line copies an example because it appears in the bible → `TEMPLATE_COPYING`.

## Voice

The copy should feel like a quiet observation from someone who knows them.
It should be: short · natural · a little understated · emotionally loaded without
saying too much · sendable · human · clean · not over-written.

## Preferred copy shapes

### Shape 1 — Deadpan observation
"she said she didn't want anything." — works when the image reveals the contradiction.

### Shape 2 — Soft reveal
"he ordered for the no too." — works when the love is in anticipation.

### Shape 3 — Tiny confession
"i knew she'd say no." — works when affection hides under humor.

### Shape 4 — Object as proof
"the second plate was never extra." — works when the prop carries the emotion.

### Shape 5 — Almost wordless
"of course." — works when the image is strong enough.

## Copy rules

- Use the fewest words that preserve the emotion.
- Prefer one good line over four average slides.
- Let the image carry the joke; let the copy carry the aftertaste.
- Do not explain the whole situation.
- Do not write punchline mechanics.
- Do not use Hinglish unless it sounds truly spoken.
- Do not transliterate Hindi for flavor.
- Do not write sitcom dialogue.
- Do not write like a meme admin.
- Do not write like a brand campaign.
- Do not write like ChatGPT.

## Human-speech test

Before approving copy, ask:
1. Would Himanshu plausibly write this?
2. Would Aachu/Zuv plausibly feel like this is them?
3. Would someone send this to their partner without embarrassment?
4. Does it sound better when spoken quietly?
5. Does the image carry half the meaning?

If any answer is no, repair.

## Hinglish policy

Hinglish is allowed only when it sounds lived-in.

- Bad: "phir mere plate pe ye haath kiska hai" / "naraaz hone ka natak karta hoon
  roz" — sounds like a staged reel script (**performative Hinglish**).
- Better: "kuch nahi chahiye, apparently."
- Even better: use simple English if Hinglish feels forced.

## Food-stealing brief examples

- Bad (banned regression): "nahi yaar, mujhe kuch nahi chahiye" /
  "phir mere plate pe ye haath kiska hai" / "naraaz hone ka natak karta hoon roz"
  / "isiliye ek extra pehle hi mangwa leta hoon" — too much dialogue, too much
  explanation, too meme-like.
- Better: "she said she didn't want anything."
- Better: "he ordered like she did."
- Better: "one plate for him. one for her no."
- Better: "the second plate was never extra."
- Strongest direction: the visual shows the second plate waiting; the copy only
  nudges the viewer toward the truth.
