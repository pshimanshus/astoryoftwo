## A Story Hook Principle

An A Story hook is not a clever first line. It is a send excuse.

The winning question is:

> Who sends this to whom, and what does sending it let them say without saying it directly?

If that answer is vague, the hook is not ready.

## Non-Rigid Use

Never let hook mechanics outrank the source winner.

Use hook language to diagnose symptoms: delayed clarity, unclear phrasing, weak
viewer fit, or low curiosity. Do not use it as a rigid approval checklist, a
template library to fill, or a reason to "fix" a winner away from what made it
work.

For source-winner remixes, the first job is to name why the winner worked:
novelty, emotional reveal, contrast, proof, comments, send behavior, caption
move, slide pacing, or visual landing. Only after that should hook mechanics
ask whether the A Story wrapper is hiding or weakening that engine.

If optimizing the hook would flatten the novelty, preserve the novelty.

## A Story Hook Libraries

Use these as format families, not templates.

### 1. Send-To-Person Accusation

Engine: a gentle call-out the sender can forward with affection as cover.

Works when: the line names a partner-specific behavior.

Avoid: mean spouse stereotypes, "tag your person" with no emotional receipt.

Example shapes:

- "send this to the person who..."
- "you did this and thought i would not notice."
- "he says he is normal. the evidence disagrees."

### 2. Small-Thing Intimacy

Engine: love is proven through tiny details the other person thinks are too
ordinary to matter.

Works when: the list feels private, not aesthetic.

Avoid: polished "small things matter" life lesson.

Example shapes:

- "i want to know the things you think are boring."
- "the tiny things were never tiny to me."
- "i remembered it because it was you."

### 3. Ordinary Future Inventory

Engine: a future made of boring daily scenes, not grand romance.

Works when: the scenes are plain enough to feel reachable.

Avoid: dreamy generic forever language.

Example shapes:

- "maybe love is just..."
- "i think i want the boring parts with you."
- "we would probably waste so many evenings so happily."

### 4. Contradiction Hook

Engine: two feelings collide in the first line: mad but caring, quiet but loud,
gone but present, boring but safe.

Works when: the scene proves both sides without explaining.

Avoid: "love is not about..." moralizing.

Example shapes:

- "we were still mad. he still..."
- "she said nothing. the cup moved closer."
- "the door was closed. his shoes were still there."

### 5. Hinglish Domestic Call-Out

Engine: one lived phrase carries the whole relationship.

Works when: the line sounds like a real couple, not internet Hinglish sprinkled
on top.

Avoid: forced slang, lazy husband-wife jokes, bitter stereotypes.

Example shapes:

- "mujhe kuch nahi hua" as a visible lie.
- "main kar lungi" as independence plus care.
- "flirting kam, faltu harkatein zyada" as playful proof.

### 6. Soft Partner Standard

Engine: a forwardable wish or thank-you disguised as a standard.

Works when: it is specific enough to point at one person.

Avoid: quote-page commands like "choose someone who".

Example shapes:

- "be with someone who notices the sentence you did not finish."
- "some people listen like it matters."
- "the right person does not make your feelings smaller."

### 7. Commentable Choice

Engine: viewers can pick themselves or their partner.

Works when: the choices are physical couple behaviors.

Avoid: personality-test slop.

Example shapes:

- "which apology are you?"
- "which one is your person after a fight?"
- "what is your couple's silent code?"

## Compatibility Screen

Before writing, answer:

1. What exact human situation is this about?
2. Who is speaking?
3. Who is supposed to receive or be tagged?
4. What would sending it let them say?
5. Is the source winner permissioned for exact reuse, premise-only, or mechanics-only?
6. Which hook family matches the topic?
7. Which tempting winner hook must not be cloned?
8. What visual proof can carry the line in the first frame?

Reject the hook if:

- The tag reason is only "relatable".
- The line could sit on any relationship quote page.
- The first frame needs a caption explanation.
- It copies a source winner's sentence spine without explicit approval.
- It has no concrete object, gesture, phrase, silence, timing, or habit.
- It sounds like the creator is teaching love instead of catching a real moment.

## Grading Rubric

Score 1 to 5:

- `send_trigger`: Would someone forward it to their person in three seconds?
- `comment_bait`: Does it invite "this is us", "guilty", or a warm argument?
- `save_reason`: Is there a line or feeling worth keeping?
- `first_frame_clarity`: Does the hook work before the caption?
- `specificity`: Is there a real behavior, phrase, object, timing, or silence?
- `freshness`: Does it avoid overused A Story and generic couple shapes?
- `source_fit`: Does it respect the permissioned source status?
- `a_story_voice`: Does it sound simple, spoken, and emotionally direct?
- `visual_proof`: Can an illustration prove the line without mood words?
- `anti_slop`: Is it free of therapy-page, quote-card, and motivational polish?

Scores are diagnostic, not mechanical law. If `send_trigger`, `specificity`,
`a_story_voice`, or `anti_slop` falls below 4, either repair the line or explain
why the source winner's causality and novelty still justify the choice. Do not
average scores into blandness.

## Output Shape

For hook generation, output:

1. Evidence basis in 3-5 bullets.
2. Compatibility screen result.
3. Hook options grouped by family.
4. Scores for each serious contender.
5. Top 3 with rewrite notes.
6. Rejected generic version.
7. Suggested first-frame visual proof.

For hook rewrite, output:

1. Grade the submitted hook.
2. Name the weakest line or word.
3. Rewrite in 5 tighter options.
4. Pick the strongest option.
5. Say what visual proof it needs.

```text
Input:

- surface: carousel / reel / caption / first-slide text
- source winner, if any:
- source status: exact reuse approved / premise-only / mechanics-only / owned A Story evidence
- target audience:
- desired emotional job: send / save / comment / tag / reply
- any locked words:

Workflow:
1. Run the anti-slop copy gate before drafting.
2. Use local A Story evidence first: outcome attribution, carousel exports,
   winner-bank artifacts, relevant run evals, and creator direction notes.
3. Rank patterns by shares per 1k reach, saves per 1k reach, comments per 1k
   reach, and follow conversion. Treat likes as secondary.
4. Build the winner causality read first: why this source worked, what novelty
   made it travel, what proof/comment/send behavior confirms it, and what would
   make the remix weaker.
5. Build a compact pattern hypothesis: observed mechanic, why it shares, how it
   adapts to this premise, what would make it generic.
6. Run the compatibility screen. Reject mismatched source formats instead of
   forcing them.
7. Use hook families only when they help preserve the winner's engine.
8. Grade each serious hook on the A Story rubric as a diagnostic, not as a rigid
   formula.
9. Rewrite anything that sounds polished, preachy, abstract, copied, or weaker
   than the winner's original landing.

Output:
- evidence basis
- compatibility result
- 12 hook options grouped by family
- score table for the best 6
- top 3 picks with why each would be sent
- rejected generic version
- first-frame visual proof for the top pick
```
