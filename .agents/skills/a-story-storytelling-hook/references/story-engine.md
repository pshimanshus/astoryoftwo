## Central Thesis

A story is a meaningful change made legible through cause and evidence.

```text
before-state
-> desire
-> obstacle or pressure
-> choice and visible receipts
-> changed state
-> earned meaning
```

Change can be external, relational, informational, or emotional. Positive and
negative trajectories both work. An unchanged scene may still be a good
vignette, observation, joke, list, or poster, but it is not yet a story.

## Change Row

Write one row before drafting:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Before | What is true at the start? |
| Desire | What does the person want, protect, avoid, or misunderstand? |
| Pressure | What makes staying the same impossible or revealing? |
| Choice | What action, refusal, mistake, or discovery moves the story? |
| After | What is observably or emotionally different? |
| Meaning | What truth has the sequence earned? |

If the after-state merely repeats the opening claim, repair the choice or the
consequence. Do not repair a missing change with prettier wording.

## Story State Card

Maintain this privately across the conversation:

```text
seed_exact:
confirmed_facts:
unknowns:
before_state:
desire:
obstacle_or_contradiction:
proof_receipts:
after_state:
open_question:
emotion_waveform:
twist_or_reframe:
payoff:
dm_send_reason:
format:
concept_lock:
copy_lock:
visual_lock:
superseded_routes:
rejection_reasons:
reopen_conditions:
```

The card is a continuity device, not a form to show the creator. Update only
the fields touched by new feedback. Preserve exact source facts and wording.

## ERCRT Translation

The supplied lesson names the diagnostic ERCRT. Use it privately and adapt it
to carousel behavior.

| Element | Carousel job | Pass signal | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emotion cycle | Create caused rises and falls | tone changes because the event changes | every slide has the same warmth or the same peak |
| Relatability | Open a reader doorway | one familiar behavior produces immediate recognition | generic "relationships are hard" language |
| Climax hint | Preview trajectory or consequence | the cover reveals what kind of change is coming | payoff is fully spoiled or no promise exists |
| Reason to continue | Make the journey matter | each beat answers one question and opens the next | decorative middle slides |
| Twisting patterns | Reframe meaning | a receipt, reversal, or consequence changes the read | random surprise or dishonest withholding |

The two Rs do different jobs: relatability earns identification; reason to
continue earns attention.

## WHW Translation

Use Why, How, What as a completeness check, not a rigid order for visible copy.

- Why: the human ache, desire, fear, joke, or relationship truth.
- How: the causal sequence and concrete receipts.
- What: the changed state, earned realization, or result.

For @a.storyof.two, begin from a felt moment rather than instructional framing.
Avoid public lines such as "Here is why" or "Three things you should do."

## Supported Carousel Architectures

### Default Seven-Beat Architecture

Map the story into the current seven-beat shape:

1. Cover: public contradiction plus an incomplete consequence.
2. Cold Open: enter the action before explaining it.
3. Mirror: let a stranger recognize their own relationship pattern.
4. Spine: reveal what the story is really testing or protecting.
5. Rhythm: escalate with distinct receipts, not restated claims.
6. Turn: change the emotional meaning of what came before.
7. Payoff: land the changed state and the one-person send thesis.

Every swipe must change at least one of these: knowledge, stakes, relationship
position, object state, emotion, expectation, or interpretation. If a slide
changes none, combine it, replace it, or cut it.

### Reflective Six-Beat Architecture

Protect this as a first-class alternate structure:

```text
Cover -> Cold Open -> Deepening -> Conflict -> Turn -> Payoff
```

Use it whenever the creator supplies or approves these roles, asks to "create
an amazing visual story for this" from a reflective premise, or the story is
stronger as a clear reflective progression than as Mirror, Spine, and Rhythm.
These names describe causal story phases, not a six-slide limit.

1. Cover: state the intimate contradiction or transformation promise.
2. Cold Open: reveal the first stabilizing truth or choice.
3. Deepening: widen the question, pressure, or meaning.
4. Conflict: show where love, certainty, or the old method stops providing an
   easy answer.
5. Turn: reinterpret the conflict without falsely solving it.
6. Payoff: land the earned change and send/save thesis.

Do not treat the six-beat route as a compressed or inferior seven-beat deck.
Do not rename `Deepening` as `Mirror` or `Conflict` as `Rhythm` in an approved
route. Each role must still have a distinct visual event and swipe reason.

#### Protected Example: Certain of you, lost in us

- Cover: I was never unsure of you. I was lost inside our life.
- Cold open: Choosing each other had answered the easiest question.
- Deepening: Then life began asking harder ones.
- Conflict: Some days, love did not tell us what to do.
- Turn: Being lost together did not mean I had chosen wrong.
- Payoff: Commitment answered who. We are still learning how.

Preserve the emotional machine: certainty in the person, uncertainty inside
the shared life, pressure without blame, a turn that separates being lost from
choosing wrong, and a payoff that resolves `who` while honestly leaving `how`
alive.

### Phase-to-Slide Expansion

An architecture names the story jobs and their order. It does not dictate one
slide per job. In the reflective route, `Deepening`, `Conflict`, and `Turn` may
each span multiple consecutive slides so the story is properly staged,
explained, and emotionally earned.

Expand a phase only when every additional slide contributes at least one new:

- question or partial answer;
- character action, reaction, decision, or change in position;
- conflict pressure, complication, cost, or consequence;
- visible receipt that changes how the viewer reads the story;
- emotional shift or fair turn setup.

Keep `Cover` focused on the central promise and use `Payoff` to answer the
central emotional question. Between them, use as many slides as the causal
story requires and no more. A nine-slide reflective route may map as
`Cover (1) -> Cold Open (2) -> Deepening (3-4) -> Conflict (5-6) -> Turn
(7-8) -> Payoff (9)`, but this is an illustration, not a new template.

Record the phase role on every slide in package artifacts. If two slides in the
same phase perform the same job, combine, replace, or cut one. Do not expand a
deck with paraphrases, decorative pauses, or generic "swipe for more" bait.

Before copy lock, a cold viewer must be able to explain who the active
characters are, what each wants or believes, what pressure changed the
situation, why the conflict grew, what caused the turn, and how the payoff was
earned without relying on the caption or private creator context. If any link
is missing, expand or repair the relevant phase instead of cramming the missing
logic into one slide.

### Question, Conflict, Character, And Answer Engine

Build retention as an honest question-and-answer chain:

1. Cover opens the central contradiction or emotional question without
   explaining away the journey.
2. Cold Open places at least two active story characters inside a concrete
   situation and makes their initial wants, beliefs, actions, or positions
   legible.
3. Deepening answers part of the opening question, then reveals a harder or
   more specific question through evidence.
4. Conflict makes competing wants, interpretations, needs, loyalties, or
   external pressures collide. Conflict need not mean cruelty or a fight.
5. Turn earns a new interpretation through action, discovery, consequence, or
   choice; it may take several scenes when the reframe needs proof.
6. Payoff answers the central emotional question and lands the changed state,
   while leaving an honest life question open when false certainty would weaken
   the story.

For couple stories, Aachu and Zuv are the default two causal characters. A
slide may show only one person, an object, or an environment, but the sequence
must preserve both characters' agency, stance, and effect on the outcome. Add
family, friends, strangers, institutions, or other characters only when they
create pressure, reveal information, force a choice, or change the result;
never add them as decorative witnesses.

Each slide must repay the previous swipe promise before opening the next one.
The viewer should continue because the unresolved human question matters, not
because the copy commands them to swipe.

### Architecture Selection

- Use the seven-beat architecture as the baseline for fresh jams when no
  structure has been supplied.
- Use the six-beat architecture when explicitly supplied, already approved, or
  naturally stronger for a linear reflective transformation.
- Treat its six named roles as ordered phases. Deepening, Conflict, and Turn
  may each span multiple slides; the final deck is not limited to six.
- Preserve the selected role names and order through copy, storyboard, prompts,
  QA, and package artifacts.
- Never add a slide only to satisfy the default; never merge beats if doing so
  erases a real change, conflict, or turn.

## Copy Delivery Review

Apply before delivering any multi-slide copy: initial working drafts, pitches
that include slide copy, requested final drafts, and repairs. Concept-only
discussion and single-line edits need only the relevant continuity and voice
checks. Keep this work private unless the creator requests an explanation.
It adds no artifact, numerical score, or creator approval gate.

### Preserve The Brief Before Writing

Recover the surviving concept, creator-selected architecture, facts, exact
locks, and wording the creator has specifically liked. Preserve liked wording
as the revision baseline even if formal copy lock has not happened; concept
approval alone does not approve every provisional line. A correction to the
middle does not authorize replacing the hook. Keep an alternative separate
unless the creator asks to change that layer.

State the unresolved relationship question and the change the ending needs to
make legible. Sketch the causal story jobs before drafting; do not fill a
fixed number of caption slots. Use `Supported Carousel Architectures` and
`Phase-to-Slide Expansion` when establishing or repairing the sequence.

### Check Instructions Against The Delivered Draft

Compare the actual words with the surviving brief. For each selected phase,
locate the exact line and planned visible event doing its job, then identify
what new knowledge, pressure, choice, relationship position, or meaning it
adds. A phase heading is not evidence. If the explanation supplies something
missing from both copy and scene, repair the draft itself.

Keep copy and images complementary: the pictures may carry the action or a
silent response. A scene plan is proposed evidence, not inspected pixels.
Missing jobs need development, not extra slides or framework labels. A clean
instruction check establishes compliance only; it does not establish good copy.

### Read As An Editor Without The Labels

Remove phase labels, author explanations, and claims that the draft works.
Read the copy continuously in the channel voice, then read it with the planned
scenes. Apply Humanizer. Name the weakest actual passage privately, quoting it
and explaining what the reader is missing. Challenge these specific risks:

- The middle inventories events while the relationship question stands still.
  Test what would be lost by cutting or moving the suspect slide.
- The claimed turn repeats a gag or swaps actors without changing its meaning.
  A reciprocal joke can earn a turn when its second occurrence reveals
  something the first could not; repetition is not automatically a failure.
- The ending resolves logistics but leaves the cover's emotional promise
  unanswered. Comic, quiet, or wordless closure can work; do not paste on a
  romantic moral or manufacture serious stakes to make it feel deeper.
- A line needs a clever-sounding explanation to feel human, merely narrates
  the picture, or could be pasted into an unrelated couple story unchanged.
  Repair the specific thought and spoken rhythm, not just its word count.

For repeated copy-quality failures, read
[Copy Taste Calibration](copy-taste-calibration.md) and its bounded positive
reference. Compare the actual candidate with one relevant creator-approved
sequence and one rejected sequence. Compare what their words and scenes
accomplish, not their props, hook syntax, slide count, or apparent performance.
Creator labels establish those examples' status, not a verdict for new work.

### Repair And Recheck

Repair the diagnosed layer, preserve untouched choices, and reread the full
sequence for the effects of the change. Do not silently abandon a liked concept
because its copy failed. Two rejected copy attempts on the same approved
premise warrant one bounded independent reader review before another rewrite
is presented, using the existing helper allowance for necessary audits.
Give that reader the creator's request, surviving choices, calibration examples
with their actual creator feedback, complete candidate, and intended scenes,
without the author's defence or expected candidate verdict. Ask for
line-specific weaknesses and missing emotional development; no numeric score
or standing agent room. If unavailable, perform a separate cold reread and
state its limits when reporting validation; do not call it independent.

If a reviewer endorses known creator-rejected copy, the review has exposed a
taste mismatch. Do not override the creator or claim the workflow is validated.
Calibrate on known cases, then assess a new draft separately; repeating the
known verdict after seeing its label is not evidence of generalization.

Review is judgment, not proof of creator taste or future performance. Do not
claim a formal eval ran unless it did. Existing eval-harness tests check review
contracts only; they do not intercept ordinary chat or certify its prose.
Deliver the repaired copy when requested, without an unsolicited self-awarded
PASS or paragraph explaining why every phase now works.

## Emotional Waveform

Prefer caused variation over a flat line or nonstop intensity. Useful shapes:

```text
recognition -> amusement -> friction -> vulnerability -> tenderness
certainty -> contradiction -> evidence -> doubt -> reframe -> clarity
ordinary -> odd detail -> escalation -> pause -> emotional reveal
```

Do not manufacture a spike on every slide. Quiet beats create contrast only
when they alter attention, proximity, or meaning.

## Viewer Participation

The supplied lesson uses shapes, clouds, car headlights, and faces as examples
of people spontaneously completing patterns. Translate that into a visual
story principle:

- show enough evidence to support one primary reading;
- leave enough room for the viewer to infer the feeling;
- prefer behavior over narrator explanation;
- use objects as receipts, not premises;
- make the open question honest and answerable;
- invite comments only when multiple readings are genuinely part of the idea.

The source calls this phenomenon `PARADOLIA`; the usual English spelling is
`pareidolia`. Treat the neuroscience language in the source as a creator's
explanatory model, not verified scientific authority.

## Continuous-Chat Protocol

Apply the hook as an explicit conversational lifecycle:

- **activate** once at the first creative request by creating the private Story
  State Card in working context;
- **refresh** after each correction by changing only affected fields;
- **resume** on follow-up by reconstructing the last surviving card from the
  conversation before adding a new route;
- **close** by retaining only the surviving decision and next action in the
  response when the creator pauses, rejects the route, or completes the
  requested handoff.

This is working conversational context, not a repository artifact. Never write
a hook-state file, start a daemon, create an agent room, or add an approval gate.

At every follow-up:

1. Recall the latest confirmed card.
2. Identify the layer changed by the creator.
3. Preserve all untouched facts and locks.
4. Mark the old route or line superseded when necessary.
5. Recompute the change row and swipe reason only where affected.
6. State a blocker only if the new instruction creates an unresolved factual
   or format conflict.

Examples of layer-local repair:

- "Make the hook stronger" changes the Cover promise, not the raw event.
- "This did not happen" changes source facts and invalidates every dependent
  beat.
- "Use 3:4 only" changes the format lock and visual plan, not the payoff.
- "Now visuals" preserves concept and copy locks and moves into direction.

## Rejection And Discussion Protocol

Run this protocol whenever the creator rejects, dislikes, dumps, compares,
questions, or continues discussing an idea, concept, story, hook, copy route,
or visual direction.

1. Preserve the creator's exact rejection or correction before interpreting
   it.
2. Identify the rejected scope: word, line, hook, scene, copy route, concept,
   emotional lane, visual system, or whole package. Do not discard approved
   layers outside that scope.
3. Mark the rejected scope superseded immediately. Stop polishing, defending,
   or quietly renaming it.
4. Record the reason when stated and the condition under which the creator
   would permit a revisit. If no reason is given, record `reason_unknown`
   rather than inventing one.
5. Continue the discussion from the latest surviving facts, locks, and open
   questions. A question, comparison, or critique does not silently unlock
   approved copy or visuals.
6. Treat explicit concept-level rejection as closed until the creator clearly
   reopens it. Do not return the same premise with cosmetic wording, a new
   title, swapped props, or a softer ending.
7. When the rejection creates a durable preference, closed lane, or reusable
   repair condition, update `memory/semantic/carousel-idea-preferences.md`
   through its `How This Ledger Learns` method during closeout. Keep transient
   wording experiments in the current Story State Card instead of bloating
   durable memory.
8. At the start of future ideation, query Agentic OS recall/search for rejected
   or cooled lanes relevant to the new seed before presenting concepts. Open
   only the matching ledger section unless a full memory audit is requested.

This protocol applies during loose thinking as strongly as during production.
The hook stays active while the creator says what is weak, asks why an idea
works, compares routes, explores alternatives, or keeps talking without asking
for final copy yet.

## Repair Table

| Symptom | Likely cause | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pretty but not a story | no changed state | define before/after and add the choice that causes movement |
| Strong cover, dead middle | no journey-level reason to continue | give each receipt a distinct consequence or question |
| Relatable but generic | mirror has no private proof | add one behavior, object movement, phrase, or cost |
| Emotional but exhausting | waveform is all peaks | add a quiet causal beat before the turn |
| Twist feels fake | information was hidden arbitrarily | plant fair evidence and make the turn a reinterpretation |
| Ending feels quoted on | payoff does not grow from the receipts | write the after-state first, then derive the thesis |
| Follow-up drifts | stale state card | apply the latest correction and mark the prior route superseded |
| Visual needs explanatory text | scene proof is weak | stage action, reaction, object state, gaze, distance, and consequence |

## Ownership And Conflict Rules

Keep one owner per concern:

| Concern | Canonical owner |
| --- | --- |
| Transformation, ERCRT/WHW, carousel arc, continuity, repairs | this file |
| Exact supplied source | `source-transcript.txt` |
| Session-level creative checks | `config/skills/creator-skill-stack.md` |
| Carousel workflow and production | `$a-story-carousel-jam` and `config/skills/carousel-jam-runtime-context.md` |
| Voice, relationship, text, identity, format, and visual hard rules | `config/rules/` |
| Historical preferences and evidence | `memory/semantic/` and `wiki/` |

Do not copy this method into the routing surfaces above. Point here, then add
only the responsibility unique to that surface.

Resolve conflicts in this order:

1. Current creator instruction and correction.
2. Nearest `AGENTS.md`.
3. `config/rules/`.
4. Current skill and workflow contracts.
5. Semantic memory and wiki evidence.
6. The transcript and older derived references.

The transcript contributes a change-and-retention lens. It cannot override
channel voice, identity, format, visual, or production rules.
