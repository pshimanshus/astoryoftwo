# Copy Taste Calibration

Use during repeated carousel-copy repair. These are labeled examples for
editorial calibration, not a phrase blacklist, numerical rubric, or held-out
test. Both rejected drafts below came from the September 30, 2026 carousel
planning conversation. The creator liked the game-night direction; copy rejection does
not retire that premise or prohibit comic endings.

## Rejected Working Copy

accidental answers helping each other, mock outrage from their teams, then a
shared team, fist bump, and their competitive expressions directed outward.

1. I wanted you to win. Against everyone except me.
2. You were terrible at explaining yourself.
3. Unfortunately, I’m very good at understanding you.
4. You said thank you. My team said other things.
5. Then it was my turn. You weren’t any better.
6. We weren’t allowed on opposite teams after that.


framework execution. A reviewer can explain its coherent comic mechanics, but
that explanation does not erase the creator's rejection of the delivered copy.

## Use The Cases Honestly

- Preserve the creator's judgments. Do not convert a model's plausible defence
  of the comic plot into a claim that the rejected writing was successful.
- Diagnose candidate-specific problems in its exact words and scenes. Do not
  demand a sentimental thesis, ban repetition, or assume that a team change is
  inherently incapable of carrying an emotional payoff.
- Use these known outcomes to calibrate the reader before reviewing a new
  candidate. A reviewer identifying them after receiving these labels has
  learned the examples; it has not demonstrated better judgment on unseen copy.
- The first blind review of these two drafts accepted the working copy and
  rejected the revision. It caught hook drift but did not reproduce the
  creator's quality boundary. Record that as a limitation of the review, not a
  successful repair or evidence that the creator was wrong.
