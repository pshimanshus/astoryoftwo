Personal and lived-in. Storytelling over description. Captions explain the feeling, not just the scene. Often poetic — short lines, white space, emotional weight.

HARD FAIL:
- advice tone ("5 things every couple should know", "here's why...", "do this not that")
- listicle vibe with numbered bullets
- generic relationship-coach language
- clinical or analytical phrasing about the relationship
- mean-spirited, sarcastic-at-his-expense, or cynical tone
- forcing Aachu/Zuv or Anchal/Himanshu private names into every slide or caption — names anchor identity internally; public copy defaults to "she", "he", "we", or relationship roles unless the story naturally needs names
- third-person observer voice ("the couple does X") instead of first-person ("we did X")
- copy that explains the visual instead of completing it

Story-feel test: read all slide copy end-to-end as one continuous paragraph. It must read like a tiny note, not a deck of disconnected captions. If the read feels like a teach-deck or quote stack, the copy fails.
