## The Hook — First 1.7 Seconds

Viewers make a continue/scroll decision in approximately **1.7 seconds**.
71% of viewers decide within the first 3 seconds. On Reels, you have 3 seconds max — effectively 2.

**A strong hook does ALL of the following:**
- Creates an open cognitive loop the brain must resolve
- Delivers specificity — one clear promise, one target audience signal
- Uses pattern interruption — unexpected vs. the content above/below in feed
- Works without sound (85% of mobile users watch on mute)
- Layers all three channels simultaneously: **visual + text overlay + verbal/audio**

**Scoring (0–10):**
- 8–10: All 3 channels fire within 2s; instant audience signal; genuine curiosity gap
- 5–7: Two of three channels work; promise clear but not viscerally compelling
- 2–4: Generic opener; no pattern interrupt; hook takes >3s to land
- 0–1: No hook present; starts with context, intro, or pleasantries

**Hook killers (instant score penalty):**
- "Hey guys, welcome back..." (greeting before value)
- Logo/title cards with no movement in frame 1
- Starting with context-setting before the payoff
- Any sentence that begins "Today I'm going to..."

## Hook Type Taxonomy

### 1. Question Hook
**Structure:** Opens with a direct or rhetorical question the target viewer is already asking.
**Mechanism:** Creates an open loop — the brain cannot resist seeking closure.
**Strong:** Question is specific and identity-confirming ("Are you a newly married couple who...")
**Weak:** Generic ("Do you want to be successful?")
**Hinglish form:** "Kya aapke husband bhi aise karte hain?" / "Kya yeh sirf hamare saath hota hai?"

### 2. Conflict / Contradiction Hook
**Structure:** Two opposing forces — person vs. person, expectation vs. reality.
**Mechanism:** Contradictions create cognitive dissonance; brain stops to resolve tension.
**Strong:** Both sides are specific and immediately relatable.
**Weak:** Vague ("Someone doubted me")
**Hinglish form:** "Maine kaha nahi, usne kaha haan — aur phir yeh hua."

### 3. Curiosity Gap Hook
**Structure:** Reveals that valuable information exists without revealing it yet.
**Mechanism:** Zeigarnik Effect — the brain is drawn toward unresolved tasks.
**Strong:** Gap is specific; payoff feels achievable within runtime.
**Weak:** Feels like clickbait with no authentic payoff.
**Hinglish form:** "Koi nahi batata yeh shaadi ke baad..."

### 4. Visual Pattern Interrupt Hook
**Structure:** The opening frame itself stops the scroll — unusual setting, strong emotion, visible result.
**Mechanism:** Visual cortex scans for novelty; incongruence triggers stop reflex.
**Strong:** Visual alone would stop a silent scroller.
**Weak:** Talking head in neutral setting, nothing unusual in frame.

### 5. Bold Statement / Contrarian Hook
**Structure:** Opens with a claim that challenges conventional wisdom.
**Mechanism:** Brain must compare claim to existing beliefs — cannot categorize without watching further.
**Strong:** Counterintuitive but plausible. "Wait, that can't be right — let me check."
**Weak:** Hyperbolic beyond credibility; generic.

### 6. Pain Point Hook
**Structure:** Names a specific frustration the target viewer has right now.
**Mechanism:** Instant recognition ("that's me") triggers engagement.
**Framework:** Identify pain → amplify → hint at solution.
**Strong:** Hyper-specific to a narrow audience.
**Weak:** Universal to the point of meaninglessness.

### 7. Reveal / Teaser Hook
**Structure:** Shows the end result in the first second, then promises to show how.
**Mechanism:** Loss aversion — viewer has already seen something they want; leaving = losing access.
**Strong:** Reveal creates genuine aspiration or surprise.
**Weak:** "Reveal" is underwhelming or over-hyped.

## Named Script Frameworks

### AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
- **Attention (0–3s):** Pattern interrupt, bold statement, or visual shock
- **Interest (4–15s):** Intriguing information, context, or surprising fact
- **Desire (16–40s):** Transformation, solution, emotional payoff
- **Action (final 5s):** Explicit CTA — follow, comment, save, share
Best for: Product/service, educational content, brand storytelling.

### PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution)
- **Problem:** Name the specific pain in the first 2 seconds
- **Agitate:** Deepen the emotional weight of that problem (stakes, consequences)
- **Solution:** Reveal the answer, technique, or insight
Best for: Tutorial, how-to, and advice content.

### BAB (Before → After → Bridge)
- **Before:** Current painful state
- **After:** Desired transformed state
- **Bridge:** Your method/insight as the path between them
Best for: Transformation stories, first home, before/after couple dynamics.

### HVC (Hook → Value → CTA)
The simplest framework for educational Reels:
- **Hook (0–3s):** Curiosity gap, question, or bold claim
- **Value (4–50s):** Deliver the promised insight, story-driven or step-by-step
- **CTA (final 3–5s):** One clear ask

## MrBeast Principles — Applied to Short-Form

From MrBeast's production handbook (verified Business Insider 2024):

### The Three Metrics That Matter
1. **CTR** — Is the concept + cover frame irresistible at first glance?
2. **AVD (Average View Duration)** — How long do people actually watch?
3. **AVP (Average View Percentage)** — What % of runtime do they complete?
Short-form equivalent: **Watch-Through Rate** and **Replay Rate**.

### First Seconds Is Everything
"The first minute of the video is the most important part." On short-form: first 3 seconds.
The hook must simultaneously set expectations AND immediately begin delivering on them.

### Crazy Progression — No Slow Burns
Compress the story. In a 30s Reel covering a 3-day situation — the first 5s should already be on day 2.
Force the viewer to feel invested before they've decided to commit.

### Re-Engagement Every 10–15 Seconds
Change something on screen every 10–15 seconds:
- New camera angle
- Text overlay pops
- B-roll cut
- Visual reveal
- Emotional beat shift

### The "Only We Can Do This" Moment
Every Reel should contain at least one moment so specific and surprising it triggers a share impulse.
For @a.storyof.two: a Kashmiri cultural detail, Himanshu's deadpan reaction, an unusual couple moment
that no other account would show the same way.

### Data Over Intuition
Retention graph reviewed second-by-second. Every dip = re-edit or cut.
Use skip rate in Insights as the equivalent signal for Reels.

## Open Loop Storytelling

**Definition:** An unanswered question or narrative tension deliberately left unresolved to compel watching.
**Mechanism:** Zeigarnik Effect — humans are driven toward unfinished tasks.

### Five Open Loop Techniques

1. **The Teaser:** Flash a high-tension moment in frame 1, cut back to beginning. "Here's what happened..." then show how you got there.
2. **The Question Plant:** Ask a specific question in the hook; defer the answer to the 80% mark.
3. **The Layered Reveal:** Introduce multiple sub-mysteries in one video; answer one, introduce another.
4. **The Cliffhanger CTA:** End on unresolved tension; direct to part 2 or follow for resolution.
5. **The "Before I Show You" Delay:** Acknowledge the payoff; insert 10–20s of necessary context before delivering it.

**Data:** Videos that open a loop within the first 10 seconds and close it in the final 20% of runtime
show a **32% average increase** in watch-through rate.

## Retention Editing Principles

### Jump Cuts
Remove all dead air, filler words, and pauses. Target average cut length: **1.5–3 seconds per clip**.
Every cut should feel like the video is moving faster than it actually is.

### The "Something Changes Every 10 Seconds" Rule
Change at least one element on screen every 10 seconds via:
- New camera angle
- B-roll overlay
- Zoom/push-in
- Text pop
- Graphic
- Sound effect
- Visual reveal

### B-Roll Strategy
- Cut to B-roll during any section where the speaker's energy drops or the visual is static
- B-roll should illustrate (not decorate) — show what is being described
- Covering a jump cut with B-roll simultaneously smooths the edit AND adds visual information

### Text Overlays as Retention Tools
- Captions add 17% watch time and 23% more likes (TikTok data, applicable to Reels)
- Reels with text overlays get **40% more saves** than those without
- Hook text in the first frame increases 3-second hold rate by **50%**
- Every text element: minimum 2 seconds on screen; minimum 36pt font equivalent; high contrast (white text, black shadow)
- Safe zone: center 80% of frame — UI covers the edges

### Pacing by Emotion, Not Clock
Cut faster during high-energy moments (revelation, conflict, humor).
Slow down slightly during emotional beats — abrupt cuts kill genuine emotional resonance.

## Seamless Loop Mechanics

**Goal:** Final frame flows invisibly back into the first frame → viewers watch 2–3 cycles without realizing.
Each replay signals deep engagement → boosts algorithmic score (watch time multiplier).

### Techniques
- **Match-cut loop:** End on the exact same action, hand position, or camera frame you opened on.
- **Audio loop:** Music or ambient sound must loop without audible click or fade.
- **Motion loop:** End motion mid-gesture that continues naturally at the opening frame.
- **Color/lighting consistency:** Mismatched lighting between end and start immediately breaks the loop.

## Cover Frame / Thumbnail Best Practices

**Technical specs:** 1080×1920px (9:16). Center 1080×1440 safe zone for grid cropping.

**Strong cover:**
- Custom frame (not auto-selected)
- Face with readable emotion
- 1-line hook text (3–6 words maximum), high contrast
- Passes "3-second grid test" — compelling as a still image in the grid

**Weak cover:**
- Auto-selected mid-action blur
- No text
- Low contrast
- Inconsistent with what the video actually delivers

**Key rule:** Cover frame and video hook must promise the same thing. Inconsistency = trust damage.

## Audio Strategy Framework

### The 70/30 Rule
- **70% trending audio:** Capture algorithmic distribution, join cultural moments, reach new audiences
- **30% original audio:** Build brand voice, establish niche authority, create remixable content

### Trending Audio Timing Rule
Use audio **while it is still in the ascending phase** — before it peaks.
Post within 24–48 hours of identifying a rising sound.
A sound with >100K Reels is likely past its algorithmic peak.

### Original Audio Advantage
When your original audio gets used by others, every Reel that uses it links back to your profile.
Deliberately engineer "remixable" sounds: catchphrase, a distinctive beat, a format-defining audio clip.

## Master Scoring Rubric — Video Concept Evaluation (0–50)

| Dimension | Max Score | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook Strength | 10 | All 3 channels (visual/text/audio) fire within 2s; specific promise; audience signal | Generic opener; no pattern interrupt; hook takes >3s |
| Hook Type Clarity | 5 | Identifiable hook type with clear mechanism | No recognizable type; muddled opening |
| Open Loop Quality | 10 | Loop opened within 10s; deferred resolution; paid off by 80% mark | No loop; or artificial |
| Retention Architecture | 10 | Visual/audio change every 10–15s; B-roll strategic; pacing matches emotion | Static visuals; no rhythm; arbitrary cuts |
| Loop/Replay Potential | 5 | End frame flows to start; audio loops cleanly; replay adds information | Hard cut ending; audio fades |
| Cover/Thumbnail Strength | 5 | Custom frame; face + emotion; 1-line hook text; grid test passes | Auto-selected frame; no text; low contrast |
| Audio Strategy Fit | 5 | Trending in ascending phase OR original with remix potential | Outdated trending; no audio strategy |

**Score interpretation:**
- 42–50: High-confidence viral concept
- 30–41: Strong concept with addressable gaps
- 18–29: Needs significant hook/structure rework
- 0–17: Rebuild from scratch
