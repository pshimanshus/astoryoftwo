## What This Episode Is

A man comes home wrecked. His wife does not ask him what happened. She slides
her plate over instead.

Then he slides it back. Because she hadn't finished it. Because she'd come home
and stopped moving too.

That's the episode: you think you are watching one person hold up another. You
are watching two people drowning quietly and feeding each other anyway. Neither
of them ever says so.

Most Indian couple content centres a woman's emotional labour, or a man as
provider, joke, or villain. Almost none of it lets a man simply be tired and be
met without being turned into a project — and almost none of it lets the care
run both directions in the same breath. That gap is why this is the pilot.

Nobody says a word for thirty seconds.

## Peak-End Discipline

The last beat is the peak, not the aftermath. An earlier draft ended on the two
of them settled on the floor with a caption describing what had just happened —
warm, and completely flat. Text that describes the previous shot is a caption,
not an ending.

The final beat must **add** information that reframes everything before it. Here
that information is: her stillness was not composure, it was the same exhaustion.
The plate coming back is the reveal, the emotional peak, and the loop point at
once.

**Loop requirement.** Shot 6's composition must rhyme with shot 1 — same floor,
same laptop glow, same plate, her in frame the same way. On replay the viewer now
reads shot 1 correctly: she has been sitting there wrecked since before he walked
in. Rewatch is the strongest ranking signal on the Shorts feed, and this earns it
structurally rather than with a gimmick.

## Shots

### Shot 1 — 0:00–0:05 · The door
**Frame.** Interior, evening. Wide. Aachu cross-legged on the living room floor,
laptop open, half-eaten plate beside her. Behind her the front door opens — a
widening wedge of warm stairwell light spilling across the floor. She turns her
head toward it.
**Motion.** Door swings; the light-wedge grows across the floorboards; her head
turns. Nothing else moves.
**Identity load.** Low — she is three-quarter rear, face partially turned.
**Text.** `Year 3. A Thursday.` — small, handwritten, lower left.
**Why.** Opens on motion, light, and a question in under three seconds. The hook
is *who just walked in*, answered immediately.

**The plant — this shot carries the reveal.** The closing line is four words and
explains nothing, so the image has to do the work. The plant must be **legible
without being emphasised**: a viewer should see it clearly, register it as
nothing, and be unable to un-see it on the second watch.

Primary detail, non-negotiable: **her bag strap is still across her chest.** She
sat down on her own living room floor and never took it off. Supporting: shoes
still on, still in the clothes she went out in, the plate half-eaten and clearly
sitting a while — fork resting, food gone cold.

Legibility is not emphasis. Compose so the strap is plainly visible at a glance
and never becomes the subject — **do not light it, centre it, push in on it, or
let the camera find it.** First watch it reads as a woman who got home a minute
ago. On the loop it reads as a woman who got home and stopped moving.

### Shot 2 — 0:05–0:10 · Him, in the doorway
**Frame.** Zuv in the doorway, bag still on his shoulder, not yet stepped in.
Medium-wide — he is deliberately small in the frame, the flat large around him.
Face readable at mid-distance: tired, slightly hollow, not performing sadness.
**Motion.** He sets the bag down. That is the entire shot.
**Identity load.** **High** — first clear Zuv face. Full photo packet required.
**Text.** none.
**Why.** Exhaustion carried by scale and posture, not by a close-up.

### Shot 3 — 0:10–0:14 · She clocks it
**Frame.** Reverse. Aachu still on the floor — she has *not* got up. Looking at
him. The read is recognition, not alarm. Understated.
**Motion.** She closes the laptop. Small, decisive.
**Identity load.** **High** — first clear Aachu face. Full photo packet required.
**Text.** none.
**Why.** The laptop closing is the whole turn. She has decided something.

### Shot 4 — 0:14–0:19 · The question that doesn't come
**Frame.** He crosses and lowers himself to the floor beside her — not the sofa,
the floor, back against it. Two-shot, wide. Her lips part very slightly: the
question forming.
**Motion.** He sits down; her mouth opens a fraction.
**Identity load.** Medium — both mid-distance, no close-up.
**Text.** none.
**Why.** The tension of the episode lives in this half-second. Do not overplay
the mouth — a fraction, or it becomes theatre.

### Shot 5 — 0:19–0:25 · The gesture
**Frame.** She doesn't ask. One hand pushes the plate along the floor toward him.
The other reopens the laptop and restarts whatever she was watching. He looks at
the plate, then takes a bite.
**Motion.** Plate slides; screen light returns to their faces; he eats.
**Identity load.** Medium — hands and mid-distance faces.
**Text.** none.
**Why.** Not-asking, and feeding him instead. The care is entirely in the hands.
Read as the ending, this is where an audience thinks the episode lands. It isn't.

### Shot 6 — 0:25–0:30 · The return
**Frame.** Wide two-shot, composed to **rhyme with shot 1** — same floor, same
laptop glow, same plate, her held in frame the way she was at the open. Both
backs against the sofa, shoulders touching. He takes one bite, then slides the
plate back to her. She takes it. Neither looks at the other. Neither has asked.
**Motion.** The plate travels back. That is the peak of the film.
**Identity load.** Low — wide, faces small, lit from below by the screen.
**Text.** Approved 2026-09-05 — four words, and they explain nothing:
```
Dono ne nahi pucha.
```
*Neither of us asked.* First person plural, so it reads as his voice without
announcing itself as narration. It names the mutuality and leaves the exhaustion
entirely to the image. **Do not extend it, gloss it, or add a second line** — the
restraint is the choice, and shot 1's plant is what pays for it.
**Why.** The plate coming back is the whole reversal. It tells you her stillness
was not composure — she had come home and stopped too, and the half-eaten plate
in shot 1 was never a casual detail. Comfort arrives mutual instead of one-way,
which is both a bigger feeling and a truer one.

The frame is also the loop point: on replay, shot 1 reads correctly for the first
time.

## Craft Constraints Checked

- **No sustained close-up on eyes at the emotional beat.** The payoff (shot 6) is
  the widest frame in the film, lit from below. Deliberate — this is where AI
  romance normally loses its audience.
- **Intimacy held wide.** Tenderness is carried by floor-sitting, touching
  shoulders, and a sliding plate. No face does the emotional work alone.
- **Dialogue: none.** One text card at the open, one at the close.
- **Continuity.** Single location, single evening, continuous time. Wardrobe and
  light state must hold across all six shots.
- **Not a template.** This episode could not be swapped with another and leave
  the series unchanged — it is a specific evening in year three.

## Production Notes

Two shots (2 and 3) carry the full identity risk; both are single-person,
mid-distance, front-facing — the easiest case for the existing stills pipeline.
The two widest shots (1 and 6) carry the least identity risk and the most
emotional weight, which is the right way round.

Route: generate all six as stills through the existing carousel path with the
photo packet attached, pass `visual-check`, then image-to-video each still into
its 5-second move. Motion briefs above are deliberately small — one moving
element per shot.
