## The Proven Viral Formula

Every carousel that worked on this channel follows the same emotional machine:

```
1. UNIVERSAL ANTI-IDEAL (hook)
   Start with something the viewer recognizes as the "wrong" version of love —
   or the thing they've been told to want but secretly know isn't real.
   "He didn't marry peace." "He didn't marry a morning person."

2. SPECIFIC AACHU PROOF (escalation)
   Concrete, behavioral, slightly embarrassing specifics that prove she is the
   anti-ideal. Not descriptions — RECEIPTS. The no-shoes exit. The ten moods
   before noon. "Mujhe kuch nahi hua" while clearly everything has happened.
   Specific enough to draw. Specific enough for people to say: "that's my wife."

3. ZUV ACTIVE RESPONSE (the turn)
   He doesn't just accept it. He DOES something. Sits closer. Brings food.
   Smiles at the chaos. Makes space. His response is specific and active —
   not generic "he's so patient." He IS patient through a real behavior.

4. TENDER THESIS (earned payoff)
   The conclusion is not a quote. It is a small, earned, specific truth that
   only makes sense because of everything that came before.
   "Love isn't finding someone calm. It's finding someone calm enough for you."
```

**This machine is mandatory.** A carousel that skips steps 2 or 3 — that has
universal hook + generic couple proof + inspirational quote — will not hit 28/30.

## The Copy Voice — What Anchal Actually Sounds Like

**Voice 1 (Vibe Girl) — 20x better performance than Voice 2:**
- First-person, immediate, slightly chaotic, self-aware
- Hinglish: "main kar lungi", "pakka", "theek hai", "bas"
- Dry deadpan: states a behavior flatly, lets it land
- No hashtag at the end of every line
- No moral. No lesson. The moment IS the content.
- Example: "me threatening to leave (again)" — nothing more needed

**Voice 2 (Storyteller) — lower reach, higher depth:**
- Third-person or narrative distance
- More lyrical, poetic, slower
- Better for Substack articles and saves than viral reaches
- Use sparingly, and never as the hook

**Rule:** 70% Voice 1 / 20% Voice 2 / 10% zero-caption (just the image speaks)

## How to Write a Hook

A hook for this channel does three things in one line:
1. Creates immediate recognition ("that's me / that's her / that's us")
2. Contains implied conflict or chaos (something went wrong, is weird, or is embarrassing)
3. Needs no explanation (no "have you ever..." setup)

**Hooks that work:**
- "me threatening to leave (again)"
- "He didn't marry peace."
- "main kar lungi" (with a visual that makes it hilarious)
- "jo tu kahegi wahi hoga" (with receipts that prove he means it)

**Hooks that fail:**
- "Sometimes love looks like..." (too abstract)
- "He is so good to me" (tells us, doesn't show us)
- "Love is choosing someone every day" (inspirational quote, not a hook)
- Anything starting with "Have you ever felt..."

## Slide Copy Rules

**Each slide does exactly one job.** Know its job before writing it:
- Slide 1: HOOK — create curiosity, imply the chaos, make them swipe
- Slide 2-3: ESCALATION — the specific receipts, the behavioral proof
- Slide 4: THE TURN — the moment everything shifts (reaction, realisation, response)
- Slide 5: THE BRIDGE — the "and then" that connects chaos to tenderness
- Slide 6+: THE PAYOFF — the small, earned, specific truth

**Copy is one or two lines max per slide.** If you're writing three lines to make
the slide work, the slide doesn't work. Simplify or split.

**The copy and visual prove the same thing simultaneously.** If the visual shows
her making tea and the copy says "she was still angry" — that's the proof. The
visual shows the action. The copy names the emotional truth. Neither repeats the other.

## Hinglish Rules

The channel speaks Hinglish as its natural register. Not forced. Not performative.
It emerges when English isn't specific enough for the feeling.

**Use Hinglish when:**
- The Hindi phrase has a texture English can't replicate ("theek hai", "pakka", "bas")
- The character's voice IS Hinglish in that moment
- The moment is domestic, intimate, small

**Don't use Hinglish when:**
- It feels like a prop to seem desi
- The emotional beat needs directness (English lands cleaner sometimes)
- It would confuse the non-Hindi reader in a way that breaks the universal mirror

## The Screenwriter Rules

Think about every carousel as a short film before writing a single line.

**Before writing anything, answer:**
1. What is the obstacle? (Not "she's chaotic" — what SPECIFIC thing went wrong)
2. What does she do? (The concrete behavior — one clear action, not a type)
3. What does he do in response? (Active, specific — not "he understood her")
4. What changes? (The reversal — something shifts, even subtly)
5. What is the earned ending? (The small truth that only works because of the above)

**If you can't answer all five, you don't have a story yet.**
You have a vibe or a theme. Go back to the moment.

**Show, don't tell in copy too:**
"He waited" is telling.
"The cup was already warm when she walked in" is showing.
The second one makes the reader feel something before they understand what they felt.

## Idea Generation — What To Look For

**Strong carousel territory for this channel:**
- A daily behavior that reveals the whole relationship (she does X, he does Y, that's them)
- A misunderstanding that resolved with tenderness (not grand gestures — small ones)
- The "wrong" reaction that turns out to be love (she's angry → actually she was scared)
- The moment she was independent and he showed up anyway
- The thing she doesn't say but he always knows
- The thing he does quietly that she only notices later
- The Kashmiri identity: specific language, food, cultural ritual — totally unowned territory
- Himanshu's POV on her chaos: what does he think? what does he notice that she misses?

**What to avoid:**
- Travel-first concepts (the place is a backdrop, not the story)
- "He's such a good boyfriend/husband" lists — green flags without a story
- Body/weight/food concepts unless the story goes much deeper than the surface
- Public spark/visibility concepts — too niche, doesn't get the tag reflex
- Photo/aesthetic-first ("the light understood her") — beauty isn't tension

## The Tag Reflex — The Real Success Metric

The carousel is working if, after the last slide, the viewer's first impulse is to
tag their partner, share to their story, or send it to someone.

**What creates the tag reflex:**
- Recognition so specific it feels like you've been spied on ("how do they know")
- An ending that gives emotional permission ("I'm not too much, I'm enough")
- A payoff that would make someone feel seen if they received it as a DM
- Copy that reads like a private joke that's somehow universal

**What kills the tag reflex:**
- A quote at the end instead of an earned beat
- Too much explanation — the viewer wants to feel it, not understand it
- Both people shown as generically perfect and in love (no tension, no recognition)
- A moral lesson at the end ("and that's why communication is important")
