## Creator expectation

When asked what the retrieved carousels taught us, a list of general lessons
is insufficient. Explain the actual visual and copy patterns, each slide's
new information and next-swipe reason, the hook, conflict or mismatch, turn,
payoff, overall theme and completeness. Compare contrasting sequences and
specific weaknesses. Preserve the exact feedback in the archive's
`creator-request.txt`. Do not make the creator repeat this depth requirement.

## Recall before applying a formula

- The recurring editorial interpretation is being known and chosen in ordinary,
  imperfect couple life. A partner's response often changes the meaning of a
  behaviour. The character need not change or be corrected; the viewer's reading
  can change instead.
- Distinguish four kinds of middle: one question carried forward; independent
  recognisable rewards; accumulating evidence; and a changing relationship
  state. Do not impose one plot or slide count on all four.
- A repeated setting can advance time, pressure, attention or interpretation.
  In the husband summons, the split word makes a swipe perform duration;
  across-room distance resolves into arrival and a reciprocal hug.
- In the original rulebook, each middle card pays with a complete miniature
  joke. The final shared blanket, snacks and phone recall earlier disputes as
  comfortable habits. A new joke at the end is a different closure mechanism.
- Copy and image can contradict, complete or reinterpret each other: denial
  versus tears; a charger question made late by a cab; water-filling made
  meaningful by visible fatigue. A generic affectionate pose does less work.
- Conflict may be expected versus actual motive, spoken versus visible truth,
  autonomy versus closeness, mine versus ours, anger versus continued care,
  social status versus belonging, or fairness versus changing capacity.
- Direct the relationship through gaze, body distance, hands, object states and
  camera purpose. A different room alone is not a different beat. Keep visual
  observations separate from a hypothetical account of an unseen action.
- Endings can reciprocate an action, gather callbacks, reinterpret earlier
  details, compress a metaphor, change membership, show time passing, or give
  the other partner a comic reply. A last romantic sentence is not by itself
  proof of completeness.
- Specific visual receipts matter: sparkler/diya in the peace/chaos close;
  forecast phone in weather-app; her face-wiping action in the listening trap;
  chair assistance in the older café; dishes comeback in the chores joke.
- Both partners need meaningful responses. Preserve agency and specificity
  without treating patient-man/chaotic-woman as a required account formula.
- Copy may move from recognisable behaviour to the other person's response,
  then to its meaning. Spoken Hinglish and reflective English should serve
  the actual situation. Short copy, long copy and wordless endings each have
  different uses; none is a universal performance rule.

## Retain the counterexamples

- Commute story: smiling at the remembered partner already turns the mood on
  slide 2; slide 4 repeats much of that result.
- Versions of us: boxes show moving and adjustment, but do not establish the
  specific replaced dream named in copy.
- House learned us: multiple object pairs must add territory, routine or
  reciprocal care rather than merely restating an already-understood merger.
- Long distance: the cover places both people together, so imagined status
  depends on text and thought bubbles.
- Photo catalogue: embrace precedes a final gym selfie; the final item adds
  personality but is less clearly a resolution to the opening shared future.

## Evidence limits that must travel with the findings

The local analysis inspected 27 mapped sequences, 158 slide positions and 153
distinct image hashes; five positions reuse images from a longer sequence.
Some mapped files are production sources, not proven published-byte matches.
The full archive differentiates fresh contact-sheet inspection from earlier
direct Instagram reviews. Historical missing posts remain unresolved.

The performance examples use saved 2026-09-12 Insights, not current values or
equal-age experiments. Husband summons had the higher observed share rate;
the original rulebook had higher save and follow rates. The later rulebook
performed differently despite the same slide count. Quiet shared objects had
less distribution but stronger response rates than that later rulebook.
These are descriptive comparisons, not identified causes.

Keep Views, Viewers, Shares, Saves and Follows distinct. Shares are not all
known private DMs. No slide retention, dwell time or abandonment was measured.
Do not infer algorithm weights, guaranteed reach, ideal length or winning art
style. Early totals cannot support settled failure verdicts. Editorial pass
scores are not performance predictions.

The older seed/prose audit has known mapping errors: Dc5eniKic95 is the
public-pressure/husband-alliance diptych, DcRQEEjCSNo is the chores joke, and
DcG9gVzCfO7 is the promise not to give up. Use recovered pixels and later
decisions, not those stale descriptions. Historical analysis does not override
current canonical art, identity, text, format or production rules.
